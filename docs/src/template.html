Abstract

Decision models, non-generative scorers that report a probability over caller-supplied options and emit no text, are increasingly deployed as routers, classifiers and guardrails. Because they generate nothing, rationales and chain-of-thought do not apply. We audit three: an encoder scorer, a decoder scorer and a closed API, entirely through interventions on the option set, using expert-annotated Bengali idioms carrying a gold figurative and a gold literal gloss. Dropping an idiom’s own literal gloss into the option list draws every arm toward it, taking 66% to 96% of each arm’s remaining Bengali errors. A model × language matrix over one shared item set shows how far this depends on the test language: every arm is 1.9 to 6.4 times more captured in Bengali than in English on the same paired entries, the commercial model’s apparent resistance is a property of its test language, and capability does not order the failure. Calibration degrades in all three arms under the same shift, and on the open arms confidence inverts, so gating on it is worse than gating at random. On a semantics-free option list, where accuracy is at chance, the best-calibrated arm still reports mean confidence 0.82. Code and data are available at github.com/sakhadib/literal-lock-in.

§ 3–4 · Dataset and method

The option list is the only intervention surface

A decision model returns a probability distribution over exactly the options the caller supplies. It produces no text and, in the commercial case, exposes no weights. Bangla Bagdhara ships, for each of its 10,361 expert-annotated idioms, both a gold figurative meaning and a gold literal gloss: a ready-made, human-certified counterfactual distractor. Given an idiom, the model chooses its figurative meaning from k = 4 candidates. The distractor family is the independent variable; everything else is held fixed.

§ 5.1 · Literal capture

The stronger model leads until the literal gloss appears

Lod-lille is the stronger model on every non-literal condition, by up to +0.133. Insert the idiom’s own literal gloss and the ordering reverses: Lod-lille falls to 0.066, half of Laya-ml’s already-below-chance 0.136. A model can be selected as “better” on a conventional benchmark while being worse at what that benchmark proxies for.

§ 5.1 · Model × language matrix

Capture tracks the language, not capability

All six cells are measured on the same 3,265 items, built by one function from one seed. Every arm is far more captured in Bengali than in English (paired McNemar p < 10−100). Lod-lille is more accurate than Laya-ml and more captured; Jev-1.13 is more accurate than both and less captured.

§ 5.2 · Calibration

Confidence is anti-diagnostic

Both open models are more confident when captured than when correct. Restricting Laya-ml’s D-LIT to its most confident 5% gives 0.007 accuracy against 0.151 at full coverage, and Lod-lille answers every item in its most confident 35% incorrectly. Their multiclass Brier scores under D-LIT, 1.030 and 1.391, are worse than the 0.750 of a uniform four-way distribution.

§ 5.3 · Semantics-free control

Confident on a list that says nothing

The O4 ablation replaces every option’s text with a bare ordinal label, so the four options are distinguishable but carry no meaning. Expected accuracy is 0.25 and whatever confidence survives is overconfidence. The best-calibrated model under real options is the worst here: Jev-1.13 reports 0.815 mean confidence at 0.254 accuracy.

§ 5.4 · Option order

Order sensitivity is architecture-specific

Every item is run under all 4! = 24 orderings of a fixed option set. The marker-based scorer changes its chosen gloss on 83.7% of items against 10.7% for the independent-option scorer and 13.3% for the commercial one. Lod-lille is documented as order-independent by construction; a single option’s own score travels 4.0×10−2 at the median item. Permuting a list also relabels it, so this measures display order and labeling together.

§ 5.5 · Unit integrity (E3)

Breaking the idiom barely registers; context does

The expression is perturbed while the option list is held fixed, under D-LIT. A model holding the idiom as a stored unit should collapse under shuffling. Neither does. The one manipulation that clearly helps both is embedding the idiom in its gold carrier sentence.
